#+TITLE: wavelet — align audio cues to visual events
#+TOOLKIT: wavelet
#+SKILL: audio-sync
#+VERSION: 0.1.0
#+DESCRIPTION: Use when a visual event (text reveal, cut, animation kick) must land on an audio cue (beat, downbeat, lyric, sfx hit). Identify the cue time, then anchor the visual via scene `start=` or tween `position=`.
#+TAGS: video audio sync beat timing music sfx voice-over
#+STATUS: experimental

* When to use this
  :PROPERTIES:
  :NETWORK: no
  :DESTRUCTIVE: no
  :REQUIRES: node>=20 wavelet ffprobe
  :END:

  An animation needs to land on a specific moment in an audio
  track — a beat, a vocal entry, a sound-effect hit, the end
  of a voiceover sentence. Wavelet authors visuals in seconds;
  your job is to translate "the kick at bar 2" into a timestamp
  and anchor a scene or tween there.

  Not for: real-time audio reactivity (wavelet renders to a
  fixed frame schedule — there's no live audio analysis at
  playback). For audio-reactive HTML in hyperframes, see the
  separate hyperframes skill.

* Mental model

  Audio in wavelet lives on its own =<gm-track>= via
  =<gm-audio>= elements. The track's =z= is required but
  doesn't visually composite (audio never paints). Per-cue
  properties:

  - =start=     — when the cue begins on the timeline (s)
  - =duration=  — how long it plays
  - =volume=    — 0.0 to ~1.0
  - =fade-in=   — ramp-up at the cue's start (s)
  - =fade-out=  — ramp-down at the cue's end (s)

  Visual events are anchored two ways:
  1. =<gm-scene start="...">= — the scene mounts at that time
  2. Tween =position= inside the scene-local timeline — the
     tween starts at that local offset (relative to scene start)

  Syncing = converting absolute audio-cue times into one or
  both of the above.

* Workflow

  #+CAPTION: 1. confirm ffprobe is available (we'll inspect audio)
  #+begin_src bash :role pre
  command -v ffprobe >/dev/null \
    || { echo "ffprobe missing — install ffmpeg first"; exit 1; }
  test -f gamut.html || { echo "no gamut.html"; exit 1; }
  #+end_src

** Step 1 — find the cue times in your audio

   #+CAPTION: total duration + sample-rate of the asset
   #+begin_src bash
   ffprobe -v error \
     -show_entries format=duration,bit_rate \
     -show_entries stream=sample_rate,channels \
     -of default=noprint_wrappers=1 \
     ./assets/music.mp3
   #+end_src

   #+CAPTION: list audio loudness peaks (rough way to find beats / hits)
   #+begin_src bash
   ffmpeg -hide_banner -i ./assets/music.mp3 \
     -af "ebur128=peak=true" -f null - 2>&1 \
     | grep -E "t:|Peak:" | head -40
   #+end_src

   For musical content, computing BPM externally is more
   reliable than peak-picking. If you know the BPM, beat times
   are straightforward:

   #+CAPTION: beat times for a known BPM (e.g. 120 BPM)
   #+begin_src bash
   # awk computes: 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 … (60/120 = 0.5s per beat)
   awk 'BEGIN { for (i=0; i<32; i++) printf "%.3f\n", i*60/120 }'
   #+end_src

   For voiceover, transcribe and use the per-word timestamps:

   #+CAPTION: transcribe the VO and emit word-level timings
   #+begin_src bash
   wavelet transcribe ./assets/vo.mp3 --out ./assets/vo.json
   # vo.json contains segments with start/end per word
   cat ./assets/vo.json | head -40
   #+end_src

** Step 2 — declare the audio cue on its own track

   #+CAPTION: add a music cue with fade-in / fade-out
   #+begin_src html
   <gm-track id="audio" z="30">
     <gm-audio asset="music" start="0s"   duration="15s"
               volume="0.8" fade-in="0.4" fade-out="1.2"></gm-audio>
     <gm-audio asset="vo"    start="2.5s" duration="8s"
               volume="1.0" fade-in="0.05"></gm-audio>
   </gm-track>
   #+end_src

   The asset bindings live at the top of =<gm-doc>= alongside
   any other media:

   #+CAPTION: register the audio assets
   #+begin_src html
   <gm-asset id="music" kind="audio" src="./assets/music.mp3"></gm-asset>
   <gm-asset id="vo"    kind="audio" src="./assets/vo.mp3"></gm-asset>
   #+end_src

** Step 3 — anchor scene starts to cue times

   The headline lands on the downbeat at 0.500s (beat 2 of a
   120 BPM song). The end card lands on the final hit at
   12.000s.

   #+CAPTION: scenes whose start= equals the audio cue time
   #+begin_src html
   <gm-scene id="headline"  start="0.500s"  duration="3.0s"> … </gm-scene>
   <gm-scene id="end-card"  start="12.000s" duration="3.0s"> … </gm-scene>
   #+end_src

** Step 4 — anchor tweens within a scene to local cues

   The scene starts at the bar; a text reveal happens 0.250s
   later (the snare). That's a tween =position= of 0.25 inside
   the scene-local timeline:

   #+CAPTION: text reveal anchored to a local offset
   #+begin_src html
   <gm-scene id="lyric" start="2.0s" duration="2.0s">
     <template>
       <h2 class="lyric">RIGHT NOW.</h2>
       <script>
         const tl = gsap.timeline({ paused: true });
         // Land "RIGHT NOW." on the snare at scene-local 0.25s
         tl.from(".lyric", {
           y: 60, opacity: 0, duration: 0.4, ease: "back.out(1.6)"
         }, 0.25);
         wavelet.registerTimeline("lyric", tl);
       </script>
     </template>
   </gm-scene>
   #+end_src

** Step 5 — duck the music under voiceover

   When VO plays, drop music volume. Today, declare the music
   cue split into segments with different volumes — there's no
   automatic ducking primitive.

   #+CAPTION: music split: full → ducked under VO → full
   #+begin_src html
   <gm-audio asset="music" start="0s"    duration="2.5s"
             volume="0.8"  fade-out="0.4"></gm-audio>
   <gm-audio asset="music" start="2.5s"  duration="8s"
             volume="0.25" fade-in="0.2" fade-out="0.6"></gm-audio>
   <gm-audio asset="music" start="10.5s" duration="4.5s"
             volume="0.8"  fade-in="0.4"></gm-audio>
   #+end_src

   Each split is its own cue against the same asset. The mixer
   sums them; cross-fading volumes across the seam reads as a
   ducked envelope. =fade-in= / =fade-out= prevent the click of
   a volume jump.

** Verify timing — render a short window + ear-check

   #+CAPTION: render only the seconds around the cue
   #+begin_src bash :role post
   wavelet render --start 0 --end 6 --out /tmp/sync-check.mp4
   open /tmp/sync-check.mp4    # macOS
   # The text reveal should hit the snare. Re-iterate offset if not.
   #+end_src

   #+CAPTION: also confirm there are no zero-volume / overlap warnings
   #+begin_src bash
   wavelet lint 2>&1 | grep -iE "audio|volume|overlap" || echo "(no audio lint issues)"
   #+end_src

* Common pitfalls

  1. *Off by one frame.* At 30 fps a frame is 33.3 ms — close
     enough that a small drift reads as wrong-feeling sync.
     If the visual feels late, subtract one frame
     (=1/fps= seconds) from the anchor.

  2. *Anchored scene start at the cue, but the enter tween
     takes 0.5s.* The viewer reads "the visual arrives 0.5s
     after the cue." Either start the scene 0.5s EARLIER (so
     the enter completes ON the cue), or use a faster-out
     ease so the perceptual peak is closer to the start.

  3. *Two audio cues at exactly the same start.* The mixer
     sums them — likely clipping. Stagger by 0.05s OR drop
     both =volume= by ~0.3.

  4. *Forgot =fade-in= / =fade-out= on a hard-edged cue.*
     Causes a "click" at the boundary. 30–80ms is enough to
     suppress it.

  5. *Used the music BPM but the track has tempo drift.* Live
     recordings + many sample packs drift. Use transcribe-style
     analysis or hand-tap the cue times instead of trusting the
     BPM grid.

  6. *Tried to react to live audio at render time.* Wavelet
     renders to a fixed frame schedule. There's no audio
     analysis in the render loop. All sync must be authored.

* Verification checklist

  - [ ] Cue times measured from the actual audio file, not guessed
  - [ ] Scene =start= or tween =position= matches the cue
  - [ ] Enter-tween timing accounts for perceptual arrival
  - [ ] Ducking envelopes present where VO meets music
  - [ ] =wavelet lint= clean on the audio track
  - [ ] Listened to a render-pass clip end-to-end
