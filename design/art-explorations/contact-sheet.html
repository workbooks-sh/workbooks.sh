What Shane is choosing & the criteria

The art style for all card art, fed by the layered art system (docs/design/brands-and-products.md §4): card visuals are composed from alpha-cut layers — setting + person cutout + product cutout — so a finite asset set yields combinatorial ad visuals. The winning style must therefore:

  • Stylized retro cartoon, never realistic — a specific period flavor, drawn-by-one-artist feel.
  • Cutout-friendly silhouettes — subjects on plain backgrounds with clean, uncropped edges that survive alpha cutout + background swap.
  • Carries both people and products — creators, founders, mascots AND jars/gadgets must read in the same vocabulary.
  • Matches the chunky-cute light UI — bright, friendly, rounded; sits well on a light interface.

Composability Verdict

Judged from the actual pixels on disk against the layered art system (§4): (a) alpha-cutout / background-swap readiness, (b) style consistency between isolated subjects and the combo scene, (c) brief failures.

sticker-pop cutout: excellent consistency: excellent

(a) Cutout / background swap
The best of the batch. Every subject ships with a thick uniform navy contour plus a white die-cut sticker border on flat cream — the cut path is literally pre-drawn. The heavy self-contained outline means cutouts will read on any swapped setting without losing edge definition. Hair on the creator is detailed but stays inside one closed silhouette.
(b) Isolated vs combo consistency
Strongest of the three. Same line weight, same candy palette, same character (brunette creator) and the same jar reappear in the combo forest scene. Honest nits: the combo collapsed the whole scene into a single die-cut sticker with a scalloped border (the model treats "sticker" as bordered object), and the creator's outfit drifted (pink cardigan → yellow cardigan). Neither matters under the layered system — you cut subjects once and composite, you don't regenerate scenes.
(c) Brief failures
None. Closest match to the chunky-cute light UI; mildly generic "kawaii sticker pack" energy is the only risk to distinctiveness.

upa-flat cutout: good one cropped silhouette consistency: excellent

(a) Cutout / background swap
Flat color fields and crisp vector-like edges alpha-cut cleanly; product and mascot are textbook cutouts. Two cautions: the creator's bell skirt runs to the bottom frame edge despite the "uncropped" instruction — her cutout would have a flat clipped base — and the style has no outlines, so cream/pale shapes (skin, dress panels) risk melting into light swapped backgrounds; settings would need enough tonal contrast.
(b) Isolated vs combo consistency
Excellent — the combo is arguably the best single image in the batch: same geometric vocabulary, grain, and palette as the isolated subjects. Character costume drifted (orange top/yellow skirt → red top/teal skirt, headband → bow), the usual generation drift the layered system exists to bypass.
(c) Brief failures
None. Most distinctive period identity of the batch; slightly more "elegant" than "chunky-cute", so it sits a half-step from the UI's toy-like roundness.

paper-cutout cutout: caveat — baked shadows consistency: excellent

(a) Cutout / background swap
Edges themselves are crisp die-cuts, but every subject has soft drop shadows baked onto the background around the silhouette. An alpha cut either carries a cream-tinted shadow halo onto the new setting or amputates the style's signature depth cue, and the baked shadow direction would fight composited scenes. Mitigation exists: cut hard to the paper edge and re-add drop shadows programmatically at composite time — which would actually reproduce the diorama look natively and makes this style conceptually the best fit for a layered system, at the cost of extra cleanup per asset.
(b) Isolated vs combo consistency
Very consistent — paper grain, layer shadows, and palette all carry into the (busy, flower-dense) combo scene; the creator and jar are recognizably the same. Minor drift in the creator's pose/outfit.
(c) Brief failures
No brief failure — charming and on-palette — but it is the highest-effort pipeline of the three because of the shadow problem.

Shortlist

1. sticker-pop — cleanest cutout affordance in the batch (outline + pre-drawn die-cut border), best match to the chunky-cute light UI, and the tightest isolated↔combo consistency. The lowest-risk choice for the layered art system as designed.
2. upa-flat — the most distinctive and ownable look; flat shapes alpha-cut trivially. Pick it if distinctiveness outweighs the two caveats (outline-less edges on light settings, enforce "full silhouette inside frame" in the style bible).
paper-cutout is the conceptual soulmate of the layered system but needs a shadow-handling pipeline; park it as the stretch option. Regenerate midcentury-ad and cereal-mascot before a final ruling if completeness matters.
