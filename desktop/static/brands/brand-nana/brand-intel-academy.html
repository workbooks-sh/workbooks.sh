    Voice Analysis

    How to read a brand's words like a brand — tagging the substrate with :insight:voice: and telling the two dominant DTC voice modes apart: athlete second-person vs heritage.

    Mara Okafor
    Lead Analyst · your instructor for this module
    last edited 2026-06-12
    4 sections · 1 quiz

    1 What "voice" actually means in the substrate

    By the time you reach this module you've already harvested the brand, passed the substrate check, and pulled the palette. Voice is the next layer — and it's the one analysts get wrong most, because it's the most tempting to free-style.

    In Brand Nana, voice is not a vibe you assert — it's a pattern you evidence. Every voice claim that lands in a deck must trace back to harvested copy: caption text, ad headlines, PDP descriptions, email subject lines. You tag those rows in the substrate with :insight:voice:, and the deck's voice section is generated from the tagged set, never from your gut. If a row isn't tagged, it can't support a slide.

    A tag is a tiny, structured record: the source row, the verbatim quote, the voice mode you're classifying it as, and a one-line warrant (why this quote is evidence of that mode). Three good tags beat thirty vague ones.

    Tagging shape :insight:voice:  →  { source, quote, mode, warrant }
    e.g. mode: athlete-2p with warrant "direct command + body verb, reader is the subject"

    2 The two modes you'll classify most

    Across DTC, two voice modes dominate and they pull in opposite directions. Most of your early classification work is just telling these apart cleanly.

    Athlete second-person

    The reader is the subject. Direct address ("you"), imperatives, body and performance verbs, present tense, short clauses. It's a coach in your ear. It sells capability and identity — who you become by using the product. Signals: you/your, command forms ("go further", "earn it"), kinetic verbs, no hedging.

    Heritage

    The maker is the subject. Third-person or brand-as-narrator, past tense and origin framing, craft nouns, materials, place, time-on-task. It's a museum placard. It sells provenance and permanence — why the thing is worth trusting. Signals: founding years, "handmade / handcrafted", named materials, geographic origin, "still / since / generations".

    Don't blur the line A brand can run both modes (a hero line in athlete-2p, the PDP body in heritage) — that's a real finding, not a failure to decide. Tag each quote as the mode it is, then report the mix. The mistake is averaging them into a mushy "confident, authentic" non-claim.

    3 Worked example — classify 3 real captions

    Here are three captions pulled straight from the Tecovas harvest substrate (substrate/tecovas/social.parquet). Read each, decide the mode, then check the warrant. This is exactly the loop you'll run on your first live brand.

    Tagged set — tecovas.com source: social.parquet · 3 of 41 rows
    @tecovasInstagram · 2026-05-19 · 9,140 likes

    "200+ hours of handcraft go into every pair. Goodyear welt, full-grain leather, made the way they were made in 1895."

    :insight:voice: mode → heritage

    Warrant: maker is the subject, not the reader. Time-on-task ("200+ hours"), named craft nouns (Goodyear welt, full-grain), and a founding-era date ("1895"). Zero second-person. Textbook provenance framing.

    @tecovasTikTok · 2026-06-09 · 412k views

    "Lace up, walk out, own the room. Your first real pair of boots is waiting — go break them in."

    :insight:voice: mode → athlete-2p

    Warrant: reader is the subject. Stacked imperatives ("lace up, walk out, own", "go break them in"), your, kinetic verbs, present tense. Sells the identity you step into — pure athlete second-person.

    @tecovasInstagram · 2026-06-04 · 6,720 likes

    "From the bootmakers of West Texas. Half the price of the old names, none of the shortcuts — boots that get better the more you wear them."

    :insight:voice: mode → heritage tilt → athlete-2p

    Warrant: dominant frame is heritage — geographic origin ("West Texas"), maker as subject ("the bootmakers"), craft contrast ("none of the shortcuts"). But the closing clause ("the more you wear them") tilts second-person, so tag the primary mode and note the tilt. This is the realistic case — most copy isn't pure.
