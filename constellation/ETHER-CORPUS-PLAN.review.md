# Adversarial Review — Ether Corpus Plan

Verified against the live repo. Several load-bearing "facts" in the plan are wrong about its own substrate, which is worse than the ML hand-waving because the gates are built on them.

## PUNCH-LIST

**1. [BLOCKER] The held-out-element gate does NOT test "learn a new verb zero-shot." It tests retrieval over the SAME 52-element distribution.** §2.2 and §9 call held-out generalization "the literal test of can it learn a verb it never saw" and "the COMPOSE proof." It is not. You withhold 20% of *your own* 52 elements whose attribute grammar (`variant`/`size`/`tone`/`disabled`), token namespace (`--work-*`), and naming convention are *identical* to the trained 80%. A model that memorizes "fill attributes from the slice, draw colors from `--work-*`" passes this without any real OOD capability. The actual target — a tenant's bespoke `prefix-*` element with a *novel attribute shape* — is relegated to "a few hand-authored synthetic-future tags" (§11.3) and is the only thing that proves the thesis, yet it's a footnote with no bar. **Fix:** Make the synthetic-future / novel-shape split the PRIMARY held-out bar (≥N examples, its own ≥80% gate), and demote same-family held-out to a sanity check. Without a real distribution shift in the held-out set, §9's keystone metric is self-fulfilling.

**2. [BLOCKER] P0's guardrail test is already broken by the plan's own numbers: registry dir has 57 JSON files, `index.json#totals.components` = 52.** The plan asserts (P0, §1, P5.4) "catalog count == `registry/index.json#totals.components` (52)" and "hides 49/54 elements." Actual: `ls registry/*.json` = 57; `totals.components` = 52; `work-flow.json` exists but `work-flow.js` lives in `src/elements/flow/`, not `work/`; `work-src`/`work-ref` have NO registry JSON at all. So "52" is not even the right denominator, and any equality guardrail will red on day one. The "54" in P0 is a third number with no provenance. **Fix:** Pin the source of truth precisely — decide whether the contract is "registry files," "indexed components," or "CEM customElements," reconcile the 57/54/52 spread, and write the guardrail against the *reconciled* number, not a guessed literal.

**3. [BLOCKER] The `example` field — load-bearing for RAFT grounding, the fixture unification (P5.3), AND the example-grounded fallback (§2.2) — does not exist in the registry today.** I checked `auth-gate.json` and `work-button.json`: keys are `attributes/events/cssVars/tokens/dependencies/...`, no `example`. P2 proposes lifting it from `// Usage:` headers, but §11.1 admits "not every element has one." So the *entire* example-grounded fallback, the golden==fixture==sampler unification, and a chunk of the deterministic gate depend on an artifact that must be hand-backfilled for 52+ elements *before* corpus generation can start — and the plan treats this as an "open question," not a P0 prerequisite. **Fix:** Promote "backfill `example` for every shipped tag" to a hard P0 task gating §4.1, with a coverage check (100% of injected tags have a valid, lint-passing example) as its own guardrail. If you can't hit 100%, the RAFT recipe degrades silently to attribute-name-only grounding.

**4. [MAJOR] design-lint does NOT lint HTML usage today — it lints element SOURCE — so the §5 deterministic gate that "the bulk of the corpus" rides on does not exist yet.** Confirmed: the only exports are `lintElementSource(name, source, contract)`, `lintThemeContrast`, `designLint(elements,...)`. There is no `lintUsage(html)`. The plan acknowledges this in P3 ("~1.5 d") but then §4.1 step 3, §5, and the *entire synthetic-floor label generator* assume `lintUsage` already works as the auto-labeler. The gate that makes the corpus "quality-bound not count-bound" is vaporware until P3 lands, and P3 is non-trivial (HTML parsing, attribute-enum lookup, raw-hex-in-inline-style detection, required-binding checks). **Fix:** Treat `lintUsage` as a BLOCKER prerequisite with its own test suite (precision/recall against a hand-labeled flawed-vs-clean set) BEFORE any synthetic generation. A label generator with low recall silently admits garbage positives; low precision discards good data. Measure it.

**5. [MAJOR] Variant enums live in `theme-contract.json#variants`, but the registry `attributes` are bare names (`["variant","size","tone","disabled"]`) — the enum gate must JOIN two files per tag, and the plan never specifies the join is reliable.** Confirmed both shapes. The §5 gate #2 ("variant ∈ `theme-contract.json#variants[tag]`") assumes every tag with a `variant` attr has a corresponding `variants[tag]` enum entry. If a tag is in the registry but absent from `theme-contract#variants` (very likely given the 57/52 mismatch), the enum check either crashes or silently passes everything — exactly the "silent off-enum fallback" failure mode it claims to catch. **Fix:** Add a contract-completeness preflight: assert every registry tag carrying enum-shaped attrs has a `variants[tag]` block; fail the build otherwise. Otherwise gate #2 has undefined behavior on the gappy tags.

**6. [MAJOR] Memorize/compose is conflated in the corpus weighting itself.** The thesis says "don't memorize the 52 elements," yet 22% (A) + 14% (B) + 12% (C) = 48% of the corpus is element composition over *your own catalog*, and §4.3 explicitly generates "all 52 elements × task types." Training 48% of examples on the fixed 52-element vocabulary IS teaching the model those 52 elements — the weights WILL absorb the catalog as a strong prior regardless of the in-context slice, because that's what gradient descent on 3,500 examples does. The held-out gate (item 1) is too weak to detect this. You cannot simultaneously saturate the corpus with the current catalog and claim the catalog isn't in the weights. **Fix:** Either (a) hold out far more than 20% and rotate which elements are "in-context only" across epochs so no single element gets a memorizable answer-frequency, or (b) accept that the 52 ARE in the weights and reframe the thesis to "compose generalizes to *adjacent* catalogs," dropping the "tenant's totally bespoke catalog, zero re-tune" promise to a hypothesis with a real OOD eval, not a guarantee.

**7. [MAJOR] Logs-flywheel chicken-and-egg is hand-waved by "production-shadow mode seeds it."** §6 bootstrap says run the bootstrapped model on existing eval packs "in production-shadow mode — those runs are the first real logs." But (a) eval-pack runs are not *real user tasks* — they're the same synthetic distribution, so the "organic" logs are just more synthetic data wearing a costume; (b) the outcome-gate requires runs that "ended in `done` with a verifiable success / live URL" — a cold-start 3B QLoRA model on a hard agentic loop will have a LOW success rate, so the highest-value error-recovery trajectories (the fix lane, weighted highest in §6.3) are exactly the ones that DON'T pass the outcome gate and never enter the corpus. You harvest only the easy wins and starve the hard tail. **Fix:** Decouple the fix-lane harvest from the outcome gate — capture *recovered* failures (error→eventual pass within a run) regardless of final task success, and explicitly state the teacher (Claude-CLI) carries 100% of fix-lane signal until organic success-rate clears a measured threshold (e.g. >40%). Don't pretend shadow runs are organic logs.

**8. [MAJOR] `_trajectory.jsonl` capture is described as "ONE opt-in artifact, wire a harvester, don't build new capture" — but it requires `slices_in_context` provenance that the runtime does not emit today.** §6.1 wants "the verbatim untruncated turn + which contract slices were in context (`slices_in_context`)." The existing `_steps.jsonl` emits tool/args/output truncated to 200B and has no notion of "which registry slices were in the prompt." Recording `slices_in_context` means threading the injected contract IDs from `work_kits.ex` injection through the agent loop into the log — that's a real plumbing change across the host, not "wire a harvester." The flywheel's join key (used by the held-out eval AND distillation) doesn't exist. **Fix:** Scope `slices_in_context` propagation as an explicit host task with its own effort estimate; verify it's even derivable post-hoc (the injected prefix is prompt-cached — is the slice list recoverable per turn?).

**9. [MAJOR] The "220× prompt-cache TTFT win" is asserted as a serving fact but is incompatible with the stated serve target (Q5_K_M on llama.cpp, CPU, $11/mo box).** §1 leans on "exploits the 220× prompt-cache TTFT win (the contract IS the cached prefix)." llama.cpp prompt caching on CPU is a *prefix-reuse* of KV cache for a SINGLE ongoing session/slot; it is not the multi-tenant, cross-request prompt cache (à la API providers) that a "220×" number implies, and on a single-core $11 box with one slot, a moving per-tenant contract prefix evicts constantly. The 220× number has no citation and no match to the serve substrate. **Fix:** Drop the "220×" claim or cite it against the *actual* llama.cpp slot/cache behavior on the target box. If the per-tenant contract must be re-prefilled per cold request on CPU, the "stable cached prefix" economic argument for compose-not-memorize partially collapses — quantify it.

**10. [MAJOR] Scale estimate: ~3,500 examples to cover 52 elements × ~10 task shapes × contract-presentation variations × fix-lane multi-turn × held-out split is thin, and the "LIMA-scale" justification is misapplied.** LIMA (1k) was *general instruction alignment* with a strong base already containing the knowledge — format installation only. Here you're asking a 3B to install (a) spine grammar, (b) theming discipline, (c) the 10-step loop, (d) read-then-use, (e) CLI verb grammar, (f) fix-lane recovery, (g) anti-fabrication — seven distinct skills — across 52 elements with hard structural constraints. 3,500 / (10 task shapes) = 350/shape, minus held-out, minus dedup. Shape H (research, 5% = 175) and I (assist, 5% = 175) are *judge-gated conversational* skills that notoriously need more data, not less. **Fix:** Either justify 3,500 with a per-shape minimum-viable-count table (and defend the thin shapes), or budget for an expansion round. "LIMA-scale" is not a justification for a 7-skill multi-constraint code agent.

**11. [MAJOR] SimPO/ORPO preference pairs are sourced from "the `work build` gate" (chosen=shorter passing, rejected=longer/failing) — this conflates two orthogonal axes and will teach length-hacking.** §7 Phase 2: "chosen = shorter passing output, rejected = longer / failing output → length-cut and correctness train as ONE signal." Binding length and correctness into one preference signal means the model cannot tell whether it was rewarded for being *correct* or for being *short*. The §11.4 mitigation (exempt verified tool-call turns) helps but doesn't fix the core: a shorter-but-also-passing vs longer-but-also-passing pair teaches "shorter is better" unconditionally, including on genuinely hard tasks needing more reasoning. **Fix:** Separate the two: correctness pairs (passing vs failing, length-matched) and length pairs (both passing, shorter preferred) as distinct preference sets, so the gradient on each axis is clean. The plan's own §11.4 risk ("learns to skip the lint/build self-check to save tokens") is the predictable result of NOT doing this.

**12. [MAJOR] `work-component` → `work-src` migration claim (P0b) is contradicted by the live tree: there is no `work-component.js` AND no `work-flow.js` in `src/elements/work/`.** P0b says "the spine compute element is `<work-src>`" and `workbook.ex:209` matching `work-component` is stale. Confirmed `workbook.ex:209` does match `work-component`, and `work-src.js` exists. BUT the plan elsewhere (§2.1, the tag table) treats `work-flow` as a live spine element, while `work-flow.js` is in `src/elements/flow/`, and the runtime `workbook.ex` docstring still describes `<work-flow>` worlds containing `<work-component>` children — i.e., the runtime's *whole structural model* is still `work-component`-based, not `work-src`-based. P0b ("1 h, match work-src AND keep work-component") understates this: the tangler, validator (`validate/1` checks `work-component`), world-detection, and comp descriptor reader are ALL `work-component`-coupled. **Fix:** Re-scope P0b from "1 h, add a tag match" to "audit every `work-component` reference in `workbook.ex` (validate, worlds, comp_of, tangle_plan) and decide the migration story." The build gate isn't just a no-op — the entire structural contract the model trains against is ambiguous between two element models.

**13. [MINOR] RAFT/Gorilla-RAT citations are directionally right but oversold as "one-directional" and "already solved."** Gorilla-RAT adapts to *argument/version evolution of APIs the model was trained on the doc-format of* — it does not demonstrate zero-shot composition of *structurally novel* elements with unseen attribute schemas, which is your tenant-catalog claim. RAFT improves grounding-given-retrieval but its papers report *non-trivial failure rates* on distractor-heavy prompts. Presenting these as "the literature is one-directional / exactly our problem already solved" is overclaiming. **Fix:** Soften to "RAFT/RAT are the right shape; transfer to novel-schema elements is the open empirical question our held-out-novel eval (item 1) must answer," not "solved."

**14. [MINOR] Gate gap: design-lint catches off-token color and bad enums in *element source*, but the model emits *workbook HTML using elements* — `lintUsage` is the thing that must catch `<grid-table variant="bordered">` and inline `style="#fff"`, and it doesn't exist (item 4).** So between now and P3, the §9 bars "Usage-lint pass-rate ≥95%," "Variant-enum ≥97%" cannot even be measured. The eval table presupposes the tool it's grading with. **Fix:** Mark §9 metrics 1–3 as BLOCKED-ON-P3 in the sequencing, so nobody reports a checkpoint against a metric whose measurement tool is unbuilt.

**15. [MINOR] Anti-forgetting budget (2% general replay, shape J) is low and unjustified for a 3B undergoing 7-skill SFT + preference-opt + optional distillation.** 2% = ~70 examples to defend ALL of the base model's general instruction-following against catastrophic forgetting across three training stages. The plan's own Phase-2.5 exists *because* forgetting is expected (IF-eval 85→45→83). Defending with 70 examples is optimistic. **Fix:** Cite the basis for 2% or raise it; tie the replay fraction to the measured IF-eval drop, not a fixed 2%.

## The 3 things most likely to sink this in practice

1. **The held-out gate is self-fulfilling (items 1 + 6).** It measures retrieval over the same distribution, not new-verb generalization, while 48% of the corpus simultaneously bakes the 52 elements into the weights. You will get a green "≥80% held-out" number, ship it, and discover the tenant-catalog/zero-re-tune promise — the entire commercial thesis — does not hold on a genuinely novel schema. The eval cannot detect its own failure.

2. **The gate is vaporware and the labels inherit its bugs (items 4 + 3 + 5).** `lintUsage` doesn't exist, the `example` field doesn't exist, and the enum check requires an unvalidated two-file join over a 57/52-mismatched catalog. The "deterministic auto-label" that makes the corpus "quality-bound" is three unbuilt/ungated artifacts deep. If `lintUsage` ships with mediocre recall, every downstream positive is suspect and you won't know until eval.

3. **The flywheel starves on the signal it needs most (item 7).** Cold-start 3B success rate × an outcome-gate that demands `done`+live-URL means the fix-lane and dead-end trajectories — the explicitly highest-weighted, only-harvestable-from-logs data — are precisely what never passes the gate. The flywheel harvests easy wins, the teacher silently carries the entire hard tail forever, and the "logs flywheel turns from day one" claim is false in the direction that matters.
