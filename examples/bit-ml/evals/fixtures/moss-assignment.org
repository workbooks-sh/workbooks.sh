The EU's AI Act implementing regulation has set a compute threshold of
10^25 FLOPs for "systemic risk" classification. This is the first hard
number in major AI regulation. The question is what it actually catches,
who it misses, and whether it has teeth.

  Article 51a — Systemic Risk Threshold
  General-purpose AI models trained using a cumulative amount of compute
  greater than 10^25 floating-point operations (FLOPs) shall be classified
  as posing systemic risk under Article 51 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689.
  The Commission shall review this threshold no later than 24 months after
  entry into force, taking into account advances in training efficiency.
  Compliance obligation for providers already deploying affected models:
  12 months from the date of publication in the Official Journal.

  Linklaters Technology Regulation Update — June 2026

  The 10^25 FLOP threshold creates a de facto "frontier tier" in EU law.
  Based on publicly available compute estimates, this captures: GPT-4 and
  successors, Gemini Ultra, Claude 3 Opus, and the forthcoming
  Grok-3 series. It does not capture: Llama-3 family, Mistral Large,
  most fine-tuned derivatives.

  The enforcement mechanism is Article 88: the AI Office may request
  compute logs from providers. The legal instrument for compelling
  disclosure from non-EU entities (primarily US hyperscalers) relies on
  the Digital Markets Act's data-sharing provisions — a mechanism that
  has not been tested in litigation.

  One senior Linklaters partner, Marta Kovacs, notes: "The compliance
  timeline is aggressive. Twelve months is shorter than the typical
  enterprise AI audit cycle."
