# EU's new AI law draws a line at 10^25 FLOPs — and most open models walk under it

**Subtitle:** The first hard number in major AI regulation lands with a 12-month compliance clock and an enforcement mechanism that has never been tested in court.

The European Union published its AI Act implementing regulation on June 7,
setting 10^25 floating-point operations as the compute threshold for
"general-purpose AI with systemic risk" — the first time a major jurisdiction
has written a specific training-compute figure into law.

The line is calibrated against GPT-4, which the regulation's recitals cite
at approximately 2.1 × 10^25 FLOPs (based on Epoch AI analysis). Models
above the threshold — GPT-4 class and newer frontier systems — face new
obligations: compute log disclosure on request, mandatory capability
evaluations, and incident reporting. Llama-3-70B, estimated at
6.4 × 10^23 FLOPs, clears the bar with room to spare and faces none of
these requirements.

The compliance window is tight: providers already deploying covered models
have 12 months from the June 7 publication date. Linklaters partner Marta
Kovacs calls the timeline "aggressive — shorter than the typical enterprise
AI audit cycle."

But the enforcement mechanism has a structural gap. Article 88 lets the EU's
AI Office request compute logs, and compelling disclosure from US
hyperscalers relies on Digital Markets Act data-sharing provisions — a legal
instrument that has never been tested in litigation.

The Commission has committed to reviewing the threshold within 24 months,
explicitly accounting for advances in training efficiency. That review clause
is an acknowledgment that the number is already a moving target: a model
trained to GPT-4 performance today can be done for a fraction of the
original compute.

## overclaim A — inflated adoption figure

Industry analysts estimate that over 40 frontier models currently deployed
globally exceed the 10^25 FLOP threshold, meaning the regulation will
immediately apply to dozens of products across US, EU, and Asian providers.

## overclaim B — from gaps section presented as fact

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google have each confirmed publicly that they are
reviewing compliance obligations under the new threshold and expect to meet
the 12-month deadline.
