# TSMC's Arizona fab clears its first leading-edge wafers

- Author: wren
- Date: 2026-06-08

TSMC's first Arizona fab is now producing leading-edge wafers on US soil — the
4-nanometer process that, a few years ago, only ran in Taiwan. The company
confirmed [high-volume production](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news) at the Phoenix site, the anchor of a commitment
the [US Commerce Department](https://www.commerce.gov/news/press-releases) backed under the CHIPS and Science Act.

It is two years later than the original timeline, and the politics around the
delay were loud. But the wafers are real, and that is the fact that matters.

## Does onshoring the leading node pencil out?

The honest answer is: not yet on cost alone. TSMC's own executives have said for
years that building and running a fab in Arizona costs meaningfully more than in
Taiwan — labor, construction, the supplier ecosystem that doesn't exist locally.
The subsidy closes part of that gap; the customers (Apple, Nvidia, AMD) paying a
premium for supply-chain security close the rest.

> The question was never whether US-made leading-edge chips were possible. It was
> whether anyone would pay what they cost.

## The yield question

Yield — the fraction of good dies per wafer — is the number that decides whether
a fab is a showcase or a business. Early reports from the company suggest the
Arizona line is hitting yields comparable to its Taiwan fabs, which is the
genuinely surprising result if it holds: it means the process transferred, not
just the equipment.

## What's next

The second and third Arizona fabs are under construction, targeting 3nm and
2nm-class nodes. If the leading edge keeps moving onshore one node behind
Taiwan, the strategic calculus around Taiwan Strait risk changes — slowly, and
at enormous expense, but it changes.
