#+TITLE: Nvidia's data-center revenue laps the rest of the segment combined
#+AUTHOR: desk
#+DATE: 2026-06-09

Nvidia's data-center business has become the whole story. In its most recent
[[https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001045810&type=10-Q][quarterly filing]], the segment again accounted for the overwhelming majority of
revenue — gaming, the franchise that built the company, is now a rounding line
next to the accelerators going into AI training clusters.

The quarter in three lines:

1. *Backlog.* Demand continues to run ahead of supply; the constraint is
   advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory, not orders.
2. *Gross margin.* Still in the mid-70s percent range — pricing power that no
   other large hardware vendor holds.
3. *Concentration.* A handful of hyperscalers make up a outsized slice of
   revenue, the footnote analysts keep circling.

* The concentration footnote

Nvidia discloses, as it must, that a small number of customers each represent
10% or more of revenue. That is the load-bearing risk: the same hyperscalers
buying GPUs by the rack are also funding their own silicon — Google's TPU,
Amazon's Trainium, Microsoft's Maia. Every in-house chip that reaches volume is
a customer partially defecting.

#+begin_quote
The bull case and the bear case are the same sentence: a few buyers account for
most of the demand.
#+end_quote

* What to watch

The number that decides the next year isn't revenue — it's whether the
hyperscalers' custom silicon takes share at the training tier, where Nvidia's
software moat (CUDA, the libraries, the installed base) is deepest. Inference is
where the moat is thinnest, and inference is where the volume is going.

prepared remarks; the 10-Q carries the language that the deck rounds off.
