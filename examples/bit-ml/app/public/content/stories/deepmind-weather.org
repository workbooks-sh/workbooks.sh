#+TITLE: DeepMind's weather model beats the supercomputers it replaced
#+AUTHOR: wren
#+DATE: 2026-06-10

A machine-learning weather model from Google DeepMind, *GenCast*, now produces
15-day global forecasts that score better than the gold-standard physics model
run by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts — and it does it
on a single TPU in about eight minutes, against the hours of supercomputer time
the numerical model demands.

The result was published in [[https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08252-9][Nature]], with the model and weights released on
[[https://github.com/google-deepmind/graphcast][GitHub]]. The claim that matters: across a 2019 test year, GenCast beat ECMWF's
ENS ensemble on 97.2% of the 1,320 verification targets the team scored.

* What the model actually does

GenCast is a /diffusion/ model — the same generative family behind image
synthesis — trained on four decades of the ERA5 reanalysis archive. Rather than
integrating the equations of atmospheric motion forward in time, it learns the
distribution of plausible next-states and samples an ensemble of them. The
ensemble is the point: weather forecasting is about probabilities, and a sharp
ensemble is worth more than a single confident wrong answer.

#+begin_quote
The supercomputer isn't gone — it produced the training data, and ECMWF still
runs the physics. What changed is the marginal cost of a forecast.
#+end_quote

* The caveats

- It is trained on reanalysis, not raw observations, so it inherits ERA5's own
  assimilation choices and biases.
- Extreme, out-of-distribution events — the storms that matter most — are
  exactly where a learned model has the least to learn from.
- "Beats ECMWF" is scored on aggregate skill metrics; operational forecasters
  weigh case-by-case reliability, which is a slower verdict.

* Who loses a contract

The quiet story is procurement. National weather services spend nine figures on
the supercomputers that run numerical models. If a TPU-minutes forecast lands
within striking distance of that skill, the next hardware refresh is a very
different conversation — and the vendors who sell those machines know it.

ECMWF, for its part, is not waiting to be disrupted: it now ships its own
[[https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/aifs-blog][AIFS]] data-driven model alongside the physics one.
